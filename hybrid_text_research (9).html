Key vocabulary

Tier 3 words — understand them before you use them

Why vocabulary matters

When you use a Tier 3 word correctly you sound like an expert. That gives you authority over your text. Understand each word and use it where it fits naturally — don't just paste it in.
Basalt
Dark volcanic rock formed when lava cools quickly at the surface
Hexagonal
Six-sided shape — the shape of the columns at Fingal Head
Columnar jointing
Cracks that form six-sided columns as lava cools from the outside inwards
Littoral rainforest
Rainforest that grows close to the sea — critically endangered in Australia
Aquatic reserve
A protected area of water where fishing and development are controlled by law
Biodiversity
The variety of living species — plants, animals, insects — in a particular area
Endemic
Found only in one specific place and nowhere else on Earth
Critically endangered
At very high risk of extinction if threats are not removed immediately
Conservation
The protection and careful management of the natural environment
Indigenous custodian
Aboriginal person with the cultural responsibility of caring for Country
Erosion caldera
A large volcanic crater shaped over millions of years by wind and water
Marine protected area
An area of ocean legally protected from fishing and harmful development

⚠️ Important — the summit track is permanently closed

The summit track at Wollumbin is permanently closed out of respect for Bundjalung custodians, who consider it a sacred Men's Place. This is not just a rule — it is an act of respect for the world's oldest living culture. The story of why the track is closed is one of the most powerful arguments you can make in your hybrid text.

The Bundjalung custodians say: “We have a responsibility for caring for Country — our environment, plants, animals, water, earth, and sky.” Think about how to honour that statement in your writing. It is thousands of years of knowledge asking to be respected. That is your most powerful argument.

Key vocabulary

Tier 3 words — understand them before you use them

Why vocabulary matters

When you use a Tier 3 word correctly you sound like an expert. That gives you authority over your text. Understand each word and use it where it fits naturally.
Caldera
A large volcanic crater formed when a volcano collapses or is eroded over millions of years
Biodiversity
The variety of living species — plants, animals, insects — found in a particular area
Gondwana
An ancient supercontinent that broke apart 180 million years ago — Australia was once part of it
World Heritage Area
A place UNESCO says has outstanding value to all of humanity and must be legally protected
Aboriginal Place
A site with special legal protection because of its cultural significance to Aboriginal people in NSW
Custodian
A person responsible for looking after something — here, First Nations people who care for Country
Songlines
Ancient paths connecting Aboriginal communities across the land through story, song and law
Sacred
Deserving deep respect — connected to spiritual or cultural law that must not be violated
Threatened species
A plant or animal at risk of extinction if its habitat is not actively protected
Subtropical rainforest
A rainforest found in warm climates, rich with diverse species — found across northern NSW
Conservation
The protection and careful management of the natural and cultural environment
Indigenous sovereignty
The right of Aboriginal people to make decisions about their own Country and culture

Remember — your text must be hybrid, not just an information report

Narrative

Draw the reader in. Use descriptive language, imagery and similes. Make them feel like they are standing at that place.

Informative

Give facts from your research. Use your Tier 3 vocabulary. Sound like an expert who knows this place deeply.

Persuasive

Make the reader care. Use modal words, emotive language, and a rhetorical question. Take a clear position.

Blend all three — don't write them in separate sections. The craft is in the weaving.
